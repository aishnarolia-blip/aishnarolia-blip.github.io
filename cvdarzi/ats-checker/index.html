How this checker works

Many companies use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) to read CVs before a person does. This checker looks at two things: how easy your CV is for software to read, and, if you add a job description, how many of the skills the job asks for your CV actually shows. You get a score out of 100 and a ranked list of what to fix first.

What we check

  • Layout: one column or several, tables, and text that software cannot pick up.
  • Headings: standard names such as Experience, Education and Skills.
  • Skills and keywords: the skills the job lists, and whether your CV shows them in the words the job uses.
  • Experience: the years the job asks for against the dated roles on your CV.
  • Contact details and length: for the country you are applying in. A photo or date of birth is expected in some countries and a mistake in others.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Check as many CVs as you like, with no sign-up. The full fix list and the CV rewriting tools are in the CV Darzi app.

Is my CV uploaded anywhere?

No. The file is read in your browser and the score is worked out there. Close the page and it is gone.

Is this the score a company's ATS will give me?

No. Employers and ATS vendors do not publish how they score, so no tool can show you theirs. This shows how well your CV fits the job and how readable it is, which is what decides most rejections before a person looks.

What is an ATS-friendly CV?

One column, real text instead of pictures, standard headings, clear dates, and the skills the job asks for written the way the job writes them. Fancy templates with columns, icons and graphics are the most common reason a good CV is read wrongly.

Can I paste a LinkedIn job link?

Not on this page: paste the job description text. The CV Darzi app reads job links from LinkedIn, Naukri, Indeed and company sites for you.
